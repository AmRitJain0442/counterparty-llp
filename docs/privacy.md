# Reviewer workflow and legitimate-interest assessment worksheet

The intended purpose is to help a reviewer verify a specific counterparty claim and avoid misdirected payments or incorrect factual conclusions. Each organization operating the service should document its purpose, necessity, balancing assessment and applicable legal basis for each desk before using real personal data. Candidate checks have a separate consent gate. An application checkbox records the operator's assertion of permission. The operator holds the underlying consent record and determines whether consent is an appropriate basis in their jurisdiction.

Prefer a registry number, company domain or aggregate movement over individual history where it answers the question. Review only a profile relevant to the claim. Missing dates and old profiles leave a question unresolved. Employment movements are dated using the next appointment and reported in groups of at least five. A coverage threshold of fifteen applies to cohort conclusions. Do not enter bank account numbers, identity documents, birth dates or unrelated details in free text.

A reviewer opens each witness, checks that the identity, time period and proposition match, and considers an ordinary alternative explanation. A payment hold calls for a callback using an independently held contact. A candidate can correct a date or explain the apparent discrepancy. A company or estate finding requests an explanation. The service performs no payments, rejections, employment actions or legal filings.

Before operational use, designate a reviewer to handle notice, access, correction, objection and deletion requests. Set a case retention period and record who can access the ledger. The local demo stores cases until the operator removes the ledger. Cloud retention and access controls belong to the deploying organization. A public receipt requires separate opt-in because public log entries persist.

See the [EU General Data Protection Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng), especially Articles 5, 6 and 22.
