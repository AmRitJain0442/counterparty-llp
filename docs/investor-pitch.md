# Counterparty LLP investor pitch

**Counterparty helps procurement teams defend supplier decisions.** For a claim about a company, it shows who observed it, on what date, and whether an independent source agrees.

## The founder pitch

> A supplier renewal goes to committee tomorrow. Someone says the supplier is shrinking. Procurement has a company profile, a headcount chart and a list of job openings. Then the CFO asks, “Did three sources see this, or are we reading the same source three times?”
>
> We built Counterparty LLP for that moment. We use Crustdata to identify the supplier and collect dated observations. Our software looks for independent records, challenges the claim, and gives the reviewer a file they can defend.
>
> We ran it on Crustdata itself. Its workforce estimate was 47 profiles on two dates. Its jobs index showed 25 postings. We filed six evidence cards, all tracing back to one provider. Counterparty filed **Insufficient witnesses**. A buyer can point to the exact limit in that file and ask the supplier for a dated staffing figure before relying on the claim.
>
> We’d start with procurement teams reviewing supplier renewals. Those teams also handle vendor bank-change requests, and our Payment desk already supports that next workflow. We’d test a price per reviewed case first. What we want from you today is two introductions to vendor-risk leads. Give each team three public suppliers and let them judge whether they can reach a defensible decision faster.

## What an investor can test

| Question | Our answer |
| --- | --- |
| **Why buy this now?** | A team can pull company and people data through an API and generate a summary in seconds. When that summary influences a renewal, someone still has to establish which entity, time period and original source support the claim. Counterparty records that work once so a second reviewer can inspect it. |
| **Who buys first?** | A procurement or vendor-risk lead who owns the renewal review. Start with a single disputed claim about a public supplier. The pilot requires a browser and a reviewer with a known comparison case. |
| **What has actually shipped?** | Four live desks cover Company, Payment, Candidate and Estate investigations. Twelve leads and 24 specialists produce inspectable reports. The filed Company case used 2.23 Crustdata credits for collection. Gemini and cloud charges are separate. A live Estate rehearsal used 40.38 credits, so desk mix matters to gross margin. |
| **How could it make money?** | Test per-case pricing against what buyers spend assembling and checking one file. Test a team subscription with included usage if the same reviewers return regularly. A pilot should measure willingness to pay and total cost per case, including provider calls, models, infrastructure and reviewer time. Pricing and margins are hypotheses. |
| **What could become hard to copy?** | Each desk encodes a different way to check evidence. A company headcount estimate and a colleague sample can describe different populations. A proposed successor company may predate the bankruptcy. Human dispositions can show which checks resolved a case and which sent reviewers down a dead end. A defensibility claim depends on repeat usage and permission to learn from those decisions. |
| **Where are the limits?** | Company and person coverage depends on Crustdata and the account’s entitlements. Repeated model opinions cannot supply an independent witness. Candidate checks require consent. The hosted ledger is shared, so the proposed pilot uses public company information. |
| **What is the next milestone?** | Two introductions, three supplier claims per team. Compare elapsed reviewer time, claims the team can defend and cases requiring another source. Then ask whether the buyer would pay to use it on the next renewal. |
